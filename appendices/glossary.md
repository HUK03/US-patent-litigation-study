# Glossary of Key Terms

> Terms are defined as used in US patent litigation practice. Definitions are intended to be practical and accessible, not exhaustive legal treatises.

---

## A

**AIA (America Invents Act)** — The Leahy-Smith America Invents Act (2011), which fundamentally reformed US patent law by switching from a first-to-invent to a first-inventor-to-file system, creating IPR/PGR proceedings, and establishing the PTAB.

**Alice/Mayo test** — The two-step framework used to determine patent eligibility under § 101, derived from *Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank Int'l* (SCOTUS 2014) and *Mayo Collaborative Services v. Prometheus* (SCOTUS 2012). Step 1: Is the claim directed to an abstract idea, natural phenomenon, or law of nature? Step 2: If yes, does the claim add an "inventive concept" that transforms the claim?

**All-elements rule** — To infringe a patent claim, every element of that claim must be present in the accused product or method. Absence of a single element defeats literal infringement.

**Anticipation (§ 102)** — A claim is invalid if a single prior art reference discloses every element of the claim as it is properly construed. Requires complete disclosure in one reference.

**APJ (Administrative Patent Judge)** — A judge at the USPTO's Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB). IPR and PGR proceedings are decided by panels of three APJs.

**Apportionment** — The principle that patent damages must be apportioned to the value of the **patented feature**, not the entire product. Required by *Garretson v. Clark* (SCOTUS 1884) and enforced by the Federal Circuit. The most important concept in modern patent damages defense.

---

## B

**BRI (Broadest Reasonable Interpretation)** — The claim construction standard formerly used by PTAB in IPR/PGR proceedings. **Replaced by the Phillips standard** in January 2018.

---

## C

**Claim chart** — A table mapping patent claim elements to corresponding features of an accused product (for infringement) or prior art (for invalidity). The primary analytical tool in patent litigation.

**Claim construction** — The judicial process of interpreting the meaning of claim terms, conducted as a matter of law by the judge (usually at a "Markman hearing"). The most important event in most patent cases.

**Complaint** — The initial pleading filed by a plaintiff to commence patent litigation. Triggers the defendant's 21-day answer deadline and the 1-year IPR petition deadline.

**Continuation patent** — A patent application that claims the same priority date as an earlier-filed parent application. Continuation patents are a major strategic tool — patent holders can file continuations with new or differently-written claims years after the original patent issues, targeting new products.

**Covenant not to sue** — An agreement by the patent holder not to assert a specific patent against the recipient. Distinguished from a license: a covenant does not exhaust the patent as to the recipient's customers (unlike a license).

---

## D

**Daubert** — *Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals* (SCOTUS 1993). Established the district court's role as "gatekeeper" to admit only reliable expert testimony under Fed. R. Evid. 702. In patent cases, used primarily to challenge damages experts for unreliable apportionment methodology.

**DJ (Declaratory Judgment)** — A pre-emptive lawsuit filed by a potential infringer seeking a court ruling that a patent is invalid or not infringed. Strategic tool to choose a favorable venue before the patent holder files suit.

**DOE (Doctrine of Equivalents)** — A doctrine allowing patent infringement even if the accused product doesn't literally meet every claim element, if the element performs substantially the same function, in substantially the same way, to achieve substantially the same result. Limited by prosecution history estoppel.

---

## E

**EMVR (Entire Market Value Rule)** — A limited exception allowing a patentee to base reasonable royalty damages on the full value of a product if the patented feature **drives customer demand** for the entire product. High bar; rarely satisfied.

**Enablement (§ 112(a))** — A requirement that the patent specification enable a person of ordinary skill in the art to make and use the full scope of the claimed invention without undue experimentation.

**Estoppel (IPR)** — Under 35 U.S.C. § 315(e)(2), an IPR petitioner who receives a Final Written Decision is estopped in district court from raising invalidity grounds based on patents or printed publications that were raised or reasonably could have been raised in the IPR. **Does not apply** to product prior art, prior use, or on-sale bar (*Ingenico v. IOENGINE*, Fed. Cir. May 2025).

---

## F

**Federal Circuit** — The US Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, which has **exclusive appellate jurisdiction** over all patent cases from US district courts. Creates nationally uniform patent law.

**Final Written Decision (FWD)** — The PTAB's decision at the conclusion of an IPR or PGR proceeding. Claims are either cancelled (unpatentable) or confirmed (patentable). Must issue within 12 months of institution.

**Fintiv** — *Apple Inc. v. Fintiv, Inc.* (PTAB 2020). Six-factor framework for PTAB's discretionary denial of IPR institution when parallel district court litigation is advanced (trial date is imminent, case is substantially underway). Major source of IPR institution denials in FY2025.

**FRAND (Fair, Reasonable, and Non-Discriminatory)** — Licensing commitment made by patent holders who declare patents essential to a technical standard. Limits royalty rates to a fair and non-discriminatory level; FRAND commitments are a defense in SEP infringement cases.

**FTO (Freedom to Operate)** — Analysis of whether a product can be made, used, or sold without infringing third-party patents. Conducted before product launch or during litigation to support design-around strategy.

---

## G

**Georgia-Pacific factors** — The 15-factor framework from *Georgia-Pacific Corp. v. U.S. Plywood Corp.* (S.D.N.Y. 1970) for calculating a reasonable patent royalty. The standard jury instruction framework for patent damages. See [Damages Defense Strategy](../07-damages-defense/damages-defense-strategy.md) for the full table.

---

## H

**Halo standard** — *Halo Electronics v. Pulse Electronics* (SCOTUS 2016). Replaced the prior *Seagate* objective recklessness test for willful infringement. Under *Halo*: willfulness requires only "deliberate or intentional" infringement (preponderance standard); enhanced damages require "egregious" conduct and are at the court's discretion.

**Hypothetical negotiation** — The legal construct for calculating a reasonable royalty: what would a willing licensor and willing licensee have agreed to in an arm's-length negotiation just before infringement began?

---

## I

**In re Cray standard** — *In re Cray Inc.* (Fed. Cir. 2017). Defined "regular and established place of business" under 28 U.S.C. § 1400(b) for patent venue purposes: requires a physical place, that is regular and established, and of the defendant (not just an employee's home).

**In re HTC Corp. rule** — *In re HTC Corp.* (Fed. Cir. 2018). Held that foreign (non-US) corporations are governed by 28 U.S.C. § 1391(c)(3) ("any district") rather than § 1400(b) for patent venue purposes. Foreign companies have **no TC Heartland protection** and can be sued in any US district.

**Invalidity contentions** — The defendant's disclosure (required by most local patent rules) identifying all prior art and invalidity grounds. Locks in the defendant's invalidity positions for the case.

**IPR (Inter Partes Review)** — A USPTO trial proceeding challenging the validity of issued patent claims on § 102/103 grounds (patents and printed publications only). Must be filed within 1 year of service of a complaint. Currently ~50% institution rate (FY2025); ~80% claim invalidation rate at FWD. See [IPR/PGR Overview](../03-post-grant-proceedings/ipr-pgr-overview.md).

**ITC (International Trade Commission)** — The US International Trade Commission. Conducts Section 337 investigations of patent infringement involving imported goods. Can issue import exclusion orders. Cases resolve in 10–12 months (no monetary damages).

---

## J

**JMOL (Judgment as a Matter of Law)** — A post-trial motion under Fed. R. Civ. P. 50(b) arguing that no reasonable jury could have reached the verdict. Must have been raised under Rule 50(a) during trial to preserve the right.

---

## L

**LOT Network (License on Transfer)** — A defensive patent aggregator: members grant each other a license to any patent that is transferred to a non-LOT-member non-practicing entity. Protects against NPE acquisitions of patents from LOT members.

---

## M

**Markman hearing** — A court hearing at which the judge determines the meaning of disputed patent claim terms. Named after *Markman v. Westview Instruments* (SCOTUS 1996). Claim construction is the judge's exclusive province (no jury).

**MFN (Most-Favored-Nation clause)** — A license provision requiring the licensor to give the licensee the benefit of any more favorable terms offered to another licensee in the future. **Dangerous for defendants** in NPE settlements — resist MFN clauses aggressively.

---

## N

**Nautilus standard** — *Nautilus, Inc. v. Biosig Instruments* (SCOTUS 2014). A patent claim is invalid for indefiniteness under § 112(b) if it fails to inform, with **"reasonable certainty,"** those skilled in the art about the scope of the invention.

**NPE (Non-Practicing Entity)** — An entity that holds patents but does not manufacture products or practice the patented technology. Also called a "patent troll" or PAE (Patent Assertion Entity). Cannot obtain injunctions post-*eBay*.

---

## O

**Obviousness (§ 103)** — A patent claim is invalid if the claimed invention would have been obvious to a **person of ordinary skill in the art (POSITA)** at the time of the invention, in view of the prior art.

**OIN (Open Invention Network)** — A defensive patent aggregator focused on Linux-related patents. Members grant each other a royalty-free license to their Linux-related patents. Free to join.

---

## P

**PAE (Patent Assertion Entity)** — A sophisticated NPE that acquires and asserts patents as a primary business model (as opposed to an operating company that also happens to hold patents).

**PGR (Post-Grant Review)** — A PTAB trial proceeding that must be filed within 9 months of patent issue. Allows broader grounds than IPR (§§ 101, 102, 103, 112). Higher institution threshold. See [IPR/PGR Overview](../03-post-grant-proceedings/ipr-pgr-overview.md).

**Phillips standard** — The claim construction standard from *Phillips v. AWH Corp.* (Fed. Cir. 2005 en banc). Claims are construed based on ordinary meaning to a POSITA in light of the specification and prosecution history. Now used in both district court and PTAB (since January 2018).

**POSITA** — Person of Ordinary Skill in the Art. The hypothetical person used to assess validity (obviousness), claim construction, and enablement. POSITA's skill level is a contested fact issue in most patent cases.

**Prior art** — Any evidence that an invention was already known or used before the patent's priority date. Includes prior patents, publications, public use, prior sales, and prior knowledge.

**Prosecution history** — The complete record of communications between a patent applicant and the USPTO examiner during patent examination. Contains claim amendments and arguments that affect claim construction and prosecution history estoppel.

**Prosecution history estoppel** — The doctrine preventing a patent holder from reclaiming scope that was surrendered during prosecution (through claim narrowing or arguments to the examiner). Limits the doctrine of equivalents.

**PTAB (Patent Trial and Appeal Board)** — The USPTO administrative body that conducts IPR, PGR, and other post-grant proceedings. Based in Alexandria, Virginia.

---

## R

**Reasonable royalty** — The minimum damages recoverable for patent infringement under § 284. Defined as the amount a willing licensor and willing licensee would have agreed to in a hypothetical negotiation just before infringement began.

**RPI (Real Party in Interest)** — The party that actually controls IPR proceedings or has an interest in the outcome. IPR petitions must disclose all RPIs; the 1-year bar and estoppel apply to all RPIs and their privies.

---

## S

**SEP (Standard Essential Patent)** — A patent declared essential to practicing an industry standard (LTE, 5G, Wi-Fi, USB, etc.). Holders of declared SEPs typically must offer licenses on FRAND terms.

**Sotera stipulation** — A stipulation filed by an IPR petitioner in district court agreeing not to pursue any invalidity ground raised or that could have been raised in the IPR proceeding. Designed to defeat PTAB's *Fintiv* concerns by eliminating the overlap between IPR and district court invalidity theories.

**Spoliation** — The destruction, alteration, or failure to preserve evidence when a duty to preserve exists. Can result in adverse inference jury instructions, monetary sanctions, or case-dispositive sanctions.

**SSPPU (Smallest Saleable Patent-Practicing Unit)** — The smallest component or unit that actually practices the patent, used as the starting point for calculating a reasonable royalty base. Prevents inflating damages by reference to the entire product's price when only one feature is patented.

---

## T

**TC Heartland rule** — *TC Heartland LLC v. Kraft Foods Group Brands LLC* (SCOTUS 2017). Held that the patent venue statute (28 U.S.C. § 1400(b)) is the exclusive basis for venue in patent cases for **US domestic defendants**. Does not apply to foreign corporations (see *In re HTC Corp.*).

---

## W

**Willful infringement** — Infringement that is "deliberate or intentional" under the *Halo* standard. A jury question; required for enhanced damages (but a willfulness finding does not automatically result in enhanced damages — that is a separate court determination requiring "egregious" conduct).

**Written description (§ 112(a))** — A patent specification must demonstrate that the inventor **actually possessed** the claimed invention at the time of filing. Relevant when broad claims are added in continuations that extend beyond what the original specification describes.
