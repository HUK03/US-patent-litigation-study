# Key Deadlines Reference

> **Quick Reference:** Patent litigation deadlines are unforgiving — several are jurisdictional and cannot be extended. Calendar these dates immediately upon receipt of a complaint. The IPR 1-year bar is the most frequently missed and most consequential deadline.

---

## 1. Statute of Limitations

| Rule | Deadline | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| **Damages look-back period** | Patent owner can only recover damages for infringement occurring within **6 years** before the complaint was filed | 35 U.S.C. § 286 |
| **Laches** (pre-*SCA Hygiene* remnant) | Laches was effectively eliminated as a defense to legal damages by *SCA Hygiene Products v. First Quality Baby* (SCOTUS 2017) | 35 U.S.C. § 282 |

## 2. District Court Litigation Deadlines

| Deadline | Timeframe | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Answer to complaint** | **21 days** from service | Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(a)(1)(A); extensions routinely granted by stipulation (30–60 days); request immediately |
| **12(b) motions (dismiss)** | Must be filed before or with the answer | Including § 101 Rule 12(b)(6) motion |
| **Rule 26(f) conference** | Before court's scheduling order deadline (typically 14–21 days after parties confer) | Sets discovery schedule |
| **Scheduling order / Rule 16 conference** | Set by court; typically 30–90 days after complaint | Controls all subsequent deadlines |
| **Infringement contentions (plaintiff)** | Per local patent rules; typically 14–45 days after Rule 16 conference | Varies significantly by district |
| **Invalidity contentions (defendant)** | Per local patent rules; typically 14–45 days after infringement contentions | Lock in prior art; failure to disclose = waiver |
| **Claim construction opening briefs** | Per scheduling order | Varies; often 30–60 days before Markman hearing |
| **Fact discovery close** | Per scheduling order | Typically 12–18 months after complaint |
| **Expert report (opening)** | Per scheduling order | Typically 30–60 days after fact discovery closes |
| **Expert report (rebuttal)** | Per scheduling order | Typically 30–60 days after opening reports |
| **Expert discovery close** | Per scheduling order | After rebuttal reports |
| **Summary judgment motions** | Per scheduling order | Typically 30–60 days after expert discovery |
| **Pre-trial order** | Per scheduling order | Typically 30–60 days before trial |
| **Motions in limine** | Per scheduling order | Typically filed with or after pre-trial order |
| **JMOL motion at close of evidence** | During trial, before case goes to jury | **Required** to preserve JMOL 50(b) post-trial |
| **Post-trial JMOL (Rule 50(b))** | **28 days** after judgment entry | Fed. R. Civ. P. 50(b) |
| **New trial motion (Rule 59)** | **28 days** after judgment entry | Fed. R. Civ. P. 59(b) |
| **Notice of appeal** | **30 days** from final judgment | 28 U.S.C. § 2107; FRAP 4(a)(1)(B); **jurisdictional — cannot be extended** |

## 3. USPTO Post-Grant Proceedings

| Deadline | Timeframe | Citation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IPR petition filing deadline** | **1 year (365 days)** from date of service of a complaint alleging infringement | 35 U.S.C. § 315(b) | **Most critical deadline**; counts from service date, not filing date; extensions not available |
| **PGR petition filing deadline** | **9 months** from patent issue date (or reissue date) | 35 U.S.C. § 321(c) | Very narrow window; unavailable for older patents |
| **Ex parte reexamination** | No deadline | 35 U.S.C. § 302 | Can be filed any time; no estoppel |
| **IPR joinder (co-petitioner)** | **1 month** after institution of original petition | 35 U.S.C. § 315(c) | |
| **PTAB POPR (Patent Owner Preliminary Response)** | **3 months** from filing of petition | 37 C.F.R. § 42.107 | Optional but standard practice to file |
| **PTAB institution decision** | Within **6 months** of petition filing (typically) | 35 U.S.C. § 314(b) | |
| **Patent Owner Response (post-institution)** | **3 months** from institution decision | 37 C.F.R. § 42.120 | |
| **Petitioner Reply** | **3 months** from POR | 37 C.F.R. § 42.23 | |
| **Final Written Decision** | Within **12 months** of institution | 35 U.S.C. § 316(a)(11) | Up to 18 months if "good cause" |
| **Appeal of PTAB FWD to Federal Circuit** | **63 days** from FWD | 35 U.S.C. § 142; Fed. R. App. P. 15 | **Different from district court 30-day rule** |

## 5. Local Patent Rule Deadlines by District

Deadlines for infringement and invalidity contentions vary significantly by district:

| District | Infringement Contentions | Invalidity Contentions | Claim Construction Briefing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N.D. Cal.** | 14 days after initial case management conference | 21 days after infringement contentions | Per Patent LR 4 schedule |
| **D. Del.** | 30 days after scheduling order | 30 days after infringement contentions | Per scheduling order |
| **E.D. Tex.** | 30 days after service of initial disclosures | 30 days after infringement contentions | Per Patent Rules |
| **W.D. Tex.** | 30 days after Rule 16 conference | 30 days after infringement contentions | Per scheduling order |
| **N.D. Ill.** | 14 days after initial status conference | 28 days after infringement contentions | Per LPR schedule |

*Always verify with current local rules — local patent rules are updated periodically.*

## 6. IPR One-Year Bar: Common Calculation Errors

The IPR one-year bar (§ 315(b)) is the most frequently miscalculated deadline. Common errors:

| Error | Correct Rule |
|---|---|
| Counting from complaint **filing** date | Count from complaint **service** date (the date the defendant was actually served) |
| Counting from receipt of demand letter | Demand letter does not start the clock; only service of a **formal complaint** does |
| Thinking a different defendant's service date applies | Each defendant's service date starts their own clock independently |
| Ignoring related companies' prior service | If the parent company and subsidiary are treated as real parties in interest, the earliest service date may apply to both |
| Assuming extensions are available | **No extensions**; the 1-year bar is statutory and absolute |

**Safe practice:** Calculate the IPR deadline on Day 1, calendar it, and begin prior art search immediately. Don't wait for infringement contentions to finalize invalidity positions before starting the IPR analysis.
