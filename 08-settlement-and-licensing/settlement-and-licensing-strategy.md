# Settlement and Licensing Strategy

> **Quick Reference:** Over 95% of patent cases settle before verdict. Settlement is not failure — it is a strategic tool. The question is not whether to settle, but when and at what value. Use litigation milestones as leverage points; use IPR proceedings as independent pressure.

---

## 1. Settlement as the Most Common Outcome

Understanding the settlement landscape helps set realistic expectations:
- **~95%** of patent cases resolve before trial (through settlement, summary judgment, or IPR cancellation)
- The average patent case settles **after Markman** but **before summary judgment**
- NPE cases settle at higher rates than competitor cases (NPEs have no ongoing business relationship to protect)
- Cases involving highly valuable core products or injunction risk are more likely to reach trial

**Settlement is not the same as weakness.** Rational settlement at the right time, at the right value, avoids:
- Trial risk (unpredictable juries can award far more than expected value calculations suggest)
- Continued litigation costs ($2M–$8M+ to trial)
- Management distraction and reputational risk
- Precedent that could affect future suits

---

## 2. When to Pursue Settlement vs. Fight to Judgment

Use this decision matrix at each major milestone:

| Factor | Fight | Settle |
|---|---|---|
| **Infringement strength** | Strong non-infringement defense | Infringement likely under any construction |
| **Invalidity strength** | Strong prior art; IPR is instituted | Patent appears valid; limited prior art |
| **Damages exposure** | Low; strong apportionment defense | High; EMVR risk; willfulness found |
| **Product importance** | Accused feature is peripheral | Accused feature is core to business |
| **Injunction risk** | NPE plaintiff (eBay bars injunction) | Competitor plaintiff; injunction realistic |
| **Precedent effect** | Settlement sets bad precedent for NPE campaign | This is an isolated dispute |
| **Cost to completion** | Remaining litigation cost is manageable | Remaining litigation cost equals or exceeds settlement demand |
| **Business disruption** | Trial would cause unacceptable disruption | Trial disruption is manageable |

**Key insight:** The rational settlement value for the **defendant** is: (Probability of losing) × (Damages if you lose) + (Remaining litigation costs). If the demand is below this number and the non-monetary terms are acceptable, settlement is rational even if you believe you will win.

---

## 3. Leverage Points: How Case Value Changes at Milestones

Patent case settlement value is not static — it shifts dramatically at key events. Defendants should seek favorable milestones before entering serious negotiations.

```
Complaint filed
    │  ← High NPE demand (inflated; before they understand your defenses)
    ▼
IPR institution (if filed) ← VALUE DROPS FOR PLAINTIFF significantly
    │    Plaintiff faces real invalidity risk; leverage shifts to defendant
    ▼
Favorable Markman ruling ← VALUE DROPS FURTHER for plaintiff
    │    Narrow claim construction limits infringement scope
    ▼
Summary judgment of non-infringement or invalidity ← PLAINTIFF MUST SETTLE OR APPEAL
    │    
    ▼
Expert reports exchanged ← Plaintiff learns your damages defense; recalibrates demand
    │    Defendant learns strength of plaintiff's damages model
    ▼
Trial ← Extreme risk for both sides; last settlement opportunity often
    │    during trial or after plaintiff rests its case
    ▼
Verdict ← Post-verdict settlement less common; focus shifts to appeal
```

**Best time for defendant to push for settlement:** After IPR institution (plaintiff's validity is under attack) AND after a favorable Markman ruling (plaintiff's infringement theory is narrowed). At this point, plaintiff's expected value has declined substantially and settlement terms are most favorable to defendant.

## 4. Valuing the Case for Settlement

### Expected Value Framework

```
Settlement Value (Defendant's rational maximum) =
  (P_liability × Damages_if_lose) + Remaining_litigation_cost

Where:
  P_liability = Probability of a liability finding (infringement + validity both upheld)
  Damages_if_lose = Expected damages award if liability is found
  Remaining_litigation_cost = Cost to complete litigation from current point

Example:
  P_liability = 40% (strong invalidity defense; moderate non-infringement risk)
  Damages_if_lose = $10M (properly apportioned royalty)
  Remaining cost = $2M (expert discovery + trial)
  
  Rational maximum = (0.40 × $10M) + $2M = $6M
```

**Caution:** This is a floor/ceiling analysis, not a negotiating position. Non-monetary factors (injunction risk, precedent, portfolio implications, management bandwidth) may justify settling at a premium or fighting at a discount.

### Non-Monetary Considerations

- **Injunction risk:** Even a small probability of an injunction against a core product warrants paying a premium in settlement (for competitor plaintiffs; NPEs essentially never get injunctions post-*eBay*)
- **Cross-license value:** For competitor disputes, the license may also give you access to the plaintiff's patents — quantify this benefit
- **Precedent and MFN risk:** Settling too cheaply creates a benchmark that attracts more NPE suits; settling with a Most-Favored-Nation clause could lock you into low rates across an entire portfolio
- **ITC track:** If an ITC investigation is running in parallel, settlement must address both forums to be complete

## 5. Types of Resolution

| Resolution Type | Description | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Lump-sum license** | One-time payment for a fully paid-up license | NPE cases; avoids ongoing royalty tracking |
| **Running royalty license** | Per-unit or percentage-of-revenue royalty | Cases where future sales are uncertain; plaintiff prefers upside participation |
| **Covenant not to sue** | Plaintiff agrees not to sue defendant (narrower than full license) | Cheaper to negotiate; be aware: covenant does not exhaust patent as to downstream customers (unlike a license) |
| **Cross-license** | Both parties license their patents to each other | Competitor disputes with mutual patent exposure |
| **Portfolio license** | License to entire patent portfolio (present and future) | Industry-wide NPE campaigns; reduces future exposure |
| **Design-around acknowledgment** | Plaintiff acknowledges the design-around does not infringe | Valuable for confirming freedom to operate going forward |

## 6. Key License Agreement Terms — Checklist

When negotiating a patent license as settlement, insist on these terms and resist the traps:

### Must-Have Provisions
- [ ] **Grant scope:** Which specific patents are licensed? Patent numbers; continuation/divisional coverage?
- [ ] **Licensed products:** Which products are covered? All current products? Future products?
- [ ] **Field of use:** Is there a field restriction? Ensure scope covers all current and planned uses
- [ ] **Territory:** Worldwide? US only? (Ensure Korean operations and imports are covered)
- [ ] **Release of past claims:** Explicit release of all claims for pre-license infringement
- [ ] **Covenant not to sue:** What specific entities are protected? Parent? Subsidiaries? Customers?
- [ ] **Patent exhaustion:** Confirm that sales by defendant exhaust the patent as to defendant's customers (important for supply chain liability)

### Provisions to Resist

| Clause | Why It's Dangerous |
|---|---|
| **Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) clause** | If you later license a competitor at lower rates, you owe a refund — this limits your future settlement flexibility and can give plaintiff retroactive benefit |
| **Broad "future patents" clause** | Licensing all future patents in a field gives the patentee an incentive to file more patents targeting your products |
| **Running royalty with audits** | Ongoing audits are disruptive and expensive; prefer lump sum or a capped running royalty |
| **Admission of validity or infringement** | Never admit validity or infringement in a settlement — standard practice is for license to be "without admission of liability" |
| **Injunction consent** | Never consent to an injunction as part of a settlement without explicit business justification |

🌏 **Korean Company License Scope Note:** Ensure the license expressly covers:
- The Korean parent company
- All US subsidiaries and affiliates
- Imports into the US of products manufactured in Korea or elsewhere
- Customers of the licensed products (patent exhaustion language)

Failure to address any of these can leave the Korean company or its downstream customers exposed to future suits.

## 7. Negotiation Process

### Stages of Negotiation

1. **Initial demand / counter-demand:** Plaintiff's opening demand is typically inflated (2–5× actual case value). Defendant's initial counter should reflect realistic assessment
2. **Information exchange:** Both sides share more about their views of the case (under confidentiality); this is also when comparable licenses may be discussed informally
3. **Mediation** (optional but highly recommended): A neutral mediator can bridge gaps that direct negotiation cannot
4. **Term sheet:** Non-binding document capturing agreed terms before definitive drafting
5. **Definitive license agreement:** Full agreement; involve patent prosecution counsel to review claim scope

### Using Mediation Effectively

JAMS and AAA patent mediators are experienced in bridging valuation gaps. Best practices:
- Select a mediator with patent law background (not just general commercial experience)
- Submit a confidential mediation brief explaining your case's strengths — this is your most direct communication with the neutral
- Use the mediator to reality-check both sides' positions — experienced mediators will tell a plaintiff if their demands are unrealistic
- Be willing to compromise on process terms (payment timing, reporting) to bridge monetary gaps

### Negotiation Leverage for Defendants

**Strongest leverage points:**
- IPR institution (validity under serious attack)
- Favorable Markman ruling (infringement theory narrowed)
- Favorable Daubert ruling excluding plaintiff's damages expert
- Summary judgment on key products or claims
- Evidence of prior comparable licenses at lower rates
- Plaintiff's own use of accused technology (suggesting the technology is widespread and inexpensive)

## 8. 🌏 Korean Company-Specific Settlement Considerations

Korean companies face a structural settlement dynamic:

**NPE industry-wide campaigns:** NPEs frequently target Korean consumer electronics companies with the same patent across a large number of cases simultaneously. If Samsung settles one NPE campaign, it potentially creates a precedent for LG, SK, Hyundai, and other Korean companies — and the NPE's next demand. Coordination (through industry associations or informal counsel coordination) to present a unified defense can be more effective than isolated settlements.

**Geographic scope of settlement:** Korean companies must ensure any settlement covers global operations. An NPE with a Korean patent family may seek to assert corresponding Korean patents in Korean courts after settling the US case — ensure the settlement agreement addresses related foreign patents or includes a covenant not to sue on the foreign family members.

**MFN risk in NPE campaigns:** When NPE campaigns target multiple large Korean companies, settling at a higher rate than competitors creates MFN liability exposure. If possible, coordinate timing and rate information with industry peers (through outside counsel, not directly between companies, to avoid antitrust issues).
