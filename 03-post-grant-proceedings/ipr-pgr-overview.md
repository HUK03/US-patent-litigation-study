# IPR and PGR: Overview of USPTO Post-Grant Proceedings

> **Quick Reference:** Inter Partes Review (IPR) is the **single most powerful validity challenge tool** for patent defendants. When instituted, approximately **80% of challenged claims are found unpatentable** at Final Written Decision (FWD). However, institution rates dropped to **50% in FY2025**, making petition quality critical.

---

## 1. Why Post-Grant Proceedings Matter for Defendants

Patent claims can only be challenged in two forums: US district courts and the USPTO Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB). PTAB proceedings — particularly Inter Partes Review (IPR) — offer critical advantages for defendants:

| Feature | District Court | PTAB (IPR/PGR) |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard of proof (invalidity)** | Clear and convincing evidence | Preponderance of the evidence |
| **Claim construction** | Phillips standard (ordinary meaning) | Phillips standard (post-2018) |
| **Examiner / Decision-maker** | Jury (or judge) | Panel of 3 APJs (Administrative Patent Judges) |
| **Discovery** | Broad (depositions, documents, interrogatories) | Very limited (depositions of expert declarants only) |
| **Timeline to decision** | 2–5 years | 12–18 months from petition filing |
| **Cost** | $2M–$8M+ | $75K–$200K per petition |
| **Invalidity grounds available** | All grounds (§§ 101, 102, 103, 112) | **IPR: § 102 and § 103 only** (printed publications and patents); PGR: broader |
| **Binding effect on district court** | Local estoppel only | Estoppel under § 315(e) if FWD issues |
| **Who can file** | Defendant (as party to litigation) | Any third party (even anonymously via ex parte reexam) |

**Bottom line:** When valid § 102/103 prior art exists, PTAB is often the most cost-effective and highest-probability route to invalidating patent claims. It should be considered in virtually every patent defense.

## 2. Inter Partes Review (IPR): Mechanics

### Who Can File
Any person or entity that is not the patent owner may file an IPR petition — including the defendant in district court litigation, a non-party seeking to invalidate the patent, or a coalition of defendants.

**Exception:** A potential petitioner is **barred** from filing an IPR if they have been served with a complaint alleging infringement of the patent more than **1 year before** the petition is filed (35 U.S.C. § 315(b)).

### Grounds Available
IPR petitions may only assert invalidity based on:
- **35 U.S.C. § 102** (anticipation) — based on **prior patents or printed publications only**
- **35 U.S.C. § 103** (obviousness) — based on **prior patents or printed publications only**

**Cannot use in IPR:** § 101 (patent eligibility), § 112 (written description/enablement/definiteness), prior public use, prior commercial sale, prior public knowledge based on products (non-printed-publication art).

### Institution Standard
PTAB will institute IPR if there is a **"reasonable likelihood that the petitioner would prevail with respect to at least one of the claims challenged."**
- FY2024 institution rate: **68%** (of petitions decided)
- FY2025 institution rate: **50%** (significant drop due to increased discretionary denials — see Fintiv below)

### PTAB Timeline
```
Day 0:    IPR Petition filed
Day ~90:  Patent Owner Preliminary Response (POPR) — optional but standard
Day ~180: Institution Decision by PTAB (may be sooner or later)
          ↓ (If instituted)
Month 9:  Patent Owner Response (POR) — full argument + evidence
Month 12: Petitioner Reply to POR
Month 14: Patent Owner Sur-reply
Month 17: Oral Argument (upon request)
Month 18: Final Written Decision (FWD) — must issue within 12 months of institution
```

### What Happens in a Final Written Decision?
- Claims can be **cancelled** (found unpatentable) — this is a final judgment binding on the world, not just the parties
- Claims can be **confirmed** (found patentable) — petitioner is estopped from challenging them again in district court on grounds raised or that could have been raised
- Claims can be **amended** (patent owner files motion to amend with substitute claims; rarely granted — only 15.6% of proposed substitute claims were approved through January 2025)

### FY2024–2025 PTAB Statistics
- **FY2024:** Of petitions reaching FWD: 26% all claims unpatentable; 6% all claims patentable; 6% mixed; 28% denied institution; 32% settled
- **FY2025:** Only 26% of all petitions reached FWD (down from 38% in FY2024); of those: 17% all claims unpatentable; **44% denied institution** (dramatic increase)
- **Claim-level invalidation at FWD:** ~80% of challenged claims found unpatentable when FWD issues (Jan 2025: 80.49%; Dec 2024: 77.57%)
- **Settlement rate:** ~25–32% of petitions resolve by settlement

## 3. Post-Grant Review (PGR): Mechanics

### Key Difference from IPR: Timing Window
PGR must be filed within **9 months of the patent's issue date** (or reissue date). This window is very narrow — most patent infringement suits are filed after the patent has been in force for years, making PGR unavailable for older patents.

### Broader Grounds Available
PGR allows challenges based on **any basis of invalidity**, including:
- § 101 (patent eligibility / Alice/Mayo)
- § 102 (anticipation) — based on any prior art, including public use, on-sale, or printed publications
- § 103 (obviousness)
- § 112 (written description, enablement, definiteness)

### Higher Institution Threshold
PTAB institutes PGR only if the petition shows it is **"more likely than not"** that at least one challenged claim is unpatentable — a higher bar than IPR's "reasonable likelihood" standard.

## 4. IPR vs. PGR vs. Ex Parte Reexamination: Comparison Table

| Feature | IPR | PGR | Ex Parte Reexamination |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grounds** | § 102/103 (patents + publications only) | Any ground (§§ 101, 102, 103, 112) | § 102/103 (substantial new question of patentability) |
| **Filing window** | Anytime up to 1 year after service of complaint | Within 9 months of patent issue | Any time (no deadline) |
| **Institution standard** | Reasonable likelihood (≥1 claim) | More likely than not | Substantial new question of patentability |
| **Estoppel on petitioner** | Yes — § 315(e): all grounds raised or reasonably could have been raised (but NOT product art post-*Ingenico* 2025) | Yes (similar to IPR) | **None** |
| **Petitioner control after filing** | Yes — petitioner participates throughout | Yes | **No** — reexam proceeds ex parte; petitioner has no further role |
| **Patent owner can add claims?** | Limited (motion to amend; rarely granted) | Limited | **Yes** — can add new, broader claims |
| **Cost** | $75K–$150K+ | $75K–$150K+ | $15K–$50K |
| **Timeline** | ~18 months from filing | ~18 months from filing | 2–4 years |
| **Anonymity possible?** | No (must identify real party in interest) | No | **Yes** — can file anonymously |

## 5. The Critical Estoppel Risk: § 315(e)

**This is the most important risk to understand before filing an IPR petition.**

Under 35 U.S.C. § 315(e)(2), a petitioner who receives a **Final Written Decision** (FWD) from PTAB is **estopped** from asserting in district court (or ITC) any invalidity ground that the petitioner:
- **Raised** in the IPR petition, OR
- **Reasonably could have raised** in the IPR

### What "Reasonably Could Have Raised" Means
*Ironburg Inventions Ltd. v. Valve Corp.* (Fed. Cir. April 3, 2023): "Reasonably could have raised" means what a **skilled searcher conducting a diligent search** could have found. The **patent owner bears the burden** of proving estoppel applies (resolving a district court split).

### The Ingenico Ruling: Product Prior Art Is NOT Estopped

*Ingenico Inc. v. IOENGINE, LLC* (Fed. Cir. **May 7, 2025**) — **Landmark decision:**

The Federal Circuit resolved a longstanding split by holding that IPR estoppel under § 315(e)(2) **does NOT apply** to:
- **Product prior art** (physical prior products)
- **Prior public use**
- **On-sale bar** (§ 102(a)(1) based on commercial activity)
- **Prior public knowledge** not captured in a printed publication

**Rationale:** Congress limited IPR grounds to § 102/103 based on "patents or printed publications" (§ 311(b)). Because product art and public use cannot be raised in IPR, they were never "raised or reasonably could have been raised" in an IPR proceeding.

**Strategic implication:** File an IPR targeting the printed-publication/patent prior art, while simultaneously maintaining **product-based prior art defenses** in district court. The IPR and district court invalidity theories can be **complementary and non-overlapping**.

### Coordinating IPR Grounds with District Court Invalidity Contentions

| Ground Type | Use in IPR? | Estopped After FWD? | Use in District Court? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prior patent (§ 102/103) | ✅ Yes | ✅ If raised or could have been raised | ⚠️ Use in IPR; estopped if IPR issues FWD |
| Prior publication (§ 102/103) | ✅ Yes | ✅ If raised or could have been raised | ⚠️ Same as above |
| Prior product / prior use | ❌ Cannot use in IPR | ✅ NOT estopped (*Ingenico* 2025) | ✅ Freely use in district court |
| § 101 (Alice) | ❌ Cannot use in IPR | ❌ Never estopped | ✅ Freely use in district court |
| § 112 (written description) | ❌ Cannot use in IPR | ❌ Never estopped | ✅ Freely use in district court |

## 6. Real Party in Interest and Privity Rules

The 1-year bar and estoppel provisions apply not only to the petitioner but also to the petitioner's **real parties in interest (RPI)** and **privies**.

**Common pitfalls for large Korean/international companies:**
- **Parent-subsidiary relationships:** If a Korean parent company is in privity with its US subsidiary, the US subsidiary's bar date (from when it was served) also applies to the parent's IPR petition
- **Indemnification agreements:** If a defendant has agreed to indemnify a customer, the customer's service date may create an RPI/privity bar for the defendant
- **Coordination with co-defendants:** Sharing invalidity theories does not automatically create privity, but close coordination (shared counsel, shared expert, shared costs) can create privity

🌏 **Korean Company Alert:** Carefully analyze whether the Korean parent, US subsidiary, and any affiliates might be treated as real parties in interest. A misstep here can result in an IPR petition being rejected as time-barred after significant investment. This analysis should be done by outside PTAB counsel before filing.
