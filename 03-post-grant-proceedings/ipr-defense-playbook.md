# IPR Defense Playbook (Respondent-Focused)

> **Quick Reference:** IPR is not just a filing decision; it is a multi-quarter defense program that must be integrated with district court and, where relevant, ITC strategy. For Korean companies, the key is to use IPR aggressively without losing flexibility in parallel forums.

---

## 1. IPR Program Objectives (Beyond "Invalidate the Patent")

Set explicit objectives at intake. A high-performing IPR strategy usually targets four outcomes at once:

1. **Institution:** pass discretionary and merits screening
2. **Merits win:** cancel asserted claims at Final Written Decision (FWD)
3. **Leverage:** increase stay or settlement pressure in parallel litigation
4. **Estoppel control:** preserve non-IPR defenses for district court/ITC

If your team only optimizes for petition filing speed, quality and estoppel planning will suffer.

---

## 2. First 30 Days: IPR Readiness Build

### Workstreams to launch immediately

- **Deadline control:** calculate § 315(b) one-year bar by served entity and affiliate
- **Claim targeting:** identify asserted claims + likely fallback continuation claims
- **Prior art funnel:** run broad search, then score references by novelty, teachability, and admissibility
- **RPI/privity review:** parent/subsidiary/customer-indemnity mapping
- **Forum coupling:** district court schedule, Fintiv risk, and potential stay windows

### 5-gate readiness test

Do not greenlight petition drafting until all are "green":

- Gate 1: clean bar-date analysis
- Gate 2: at least one institution-grade lead ground
- Gate 3: expert candidate available and conflict-cleared
- Gate 4: litigation positions aligned with trial team
- Gate 5: estoppel plan approved by in-house lead

---

## 3. Prior Art Portfolio Design (Lead, Backup, and Reserve)

Treat prior art like a portfolio, not a single chart.

### Reference tiers

- **Lead references:** best § 102/103 merits and strongest documentary dates
- **Backup references:** similar technical mapping, different provenance to reduce single-point failure
- **Reserve references:** held for contingencies (institution denial, claim construction shifts, expert attack)

### Quality scoring model (example)

| Metric | Weight | Questions |
|---|---:|---|
| Element coverage | 35% | Does each limitation map cleanly and expressly? |
| Date/authentication strength | 25% | Is publication date provable and attack-resistant? |
| Non-cumulativeness | 20% | Is this materially different from prosecution record art? |
| Teachability to APJs | 20% | Can the theory be explained clearly within word limits? |

Use numeric scoring to avoid "favorite art" bias by individual engineers or search vendors.

---

## 4. Petition Architecture That Survives Institution Review

### Drafting principles

- Put strongest ground first; do not bury merits
- Use consistent POSITA definition across all grounds
- Avoid over-aggressive constructions that undermine credibility
- Tie every conclusion to an exhibit and declaration paragraph
- Keep the story coherent: problem → prior art solution → claim overlap

### Common denial triggers to preempt

- conclusory motivation-to-combine language
- inadequate RPI disclosure
- cumulative art without distinction from examined record
- obvious copy-paste charts lacking narrative integration

Institution is won by clarity and evidence discipline, not by volume.

---

## 5. Fintiv-Resilient Filing Strategy

Discretionary denial risk remains a practical reality in parallel litigation contexts.

### Defensive playbook

- file as early as feasible after service (quality still first)
- maintain a stay-ready package (draft motion + timeline narrative)
- coordinate district invalidity contentions to manage overlap optics
- prepare stipulation strategy where appropriate to mitigate overlap concerns

### Internal timing policy (recommended)

- **Target petition readiness:** within 90–150 days from complaint service
- **Absolute internal red zone:** if first full draft is not done by day 240, escalate to executive legal review

---

## 6. Estoppel-Limited Design After FWD

Even in a successful program, plan for both outcomes.

### If FWD is favorable (claims canceled)

- trigger immediate litigation-disposition motions in parallel forums
- evaluate related family patents for follow-on petitions
- preserve all record materials for expected Federal Circuit appeal

### If FWD is unfavorable (claims survive)

- activate estoppel matrix for each asserted claim/ground
- pivot to non-estopped theories (e.g., product/prior-use/on-sale frameworks, plus non-IPR statutory grounds where available)
- update expert sequence and trial themes within 2 weeks of FWD

### Estoppel management tool

Create a claim-by-claim table tracking:

- IPR grounds actually raised
- grounds potentially deemed reasonably raisable
- non-estopped defenses remaining by forum

Do this before institution, then refresh at FWD.

---

## 7. Korean Company-Specific Execution Risks

🌏 **Risk 1: Affiliate timing mismatch**  
Different entities (Korean parent, US subsidiary, distributors) may have different service dates; the earliest relevant date can control your bar analysis.

🌏 **Risk 2: Evidence localization lag**  
Critical technical documents often reside in Korean systems. Delay in translation/authentication can degrade petition quality.

🌏 **Risk 3: Split counsel misalignment**  
PTAB team and district team may diverge on claim language or theory. Require one integrated theory owner across forums.

🌏 **Risk 4: Budget underestimation**  
Single-petition assumptions fail in multi-claim, multi-patent cases. Pre-approve multi-petition contingencies and expert bandwidth.

## 8. IPR Governance: Weekly Cadence for In-House Teams

### Weekly steering agenda (45 minutes)

1. Deadline and dependency review
2. Prior art status by claim
3. Expert draft progress and open assumptions
4. RPI/privity issues and customer-indemnity impacts
5. District/ITC coordination and messaging consistency
6. Settlement posture and business triggers

### Dashboard KPIs

- days to bar date (global and entity-specific)
- institution-grade grounds count by patent
- % of challenged limitations with primary and backup citation support
- petition draft completion rate
- unresolved estoppel-risk issues
- stay-likelihood status (high/medium/low)

---

## 9. 12-Month Integrated Timeline (Complaint to Institution + Trial Sync)

```
Month 0–1: Intake, bar-date mapping, prior art sprint
Month 2–3: Ground selection, expert onboarding, petition architecture
Month 4–5: Drafting, declaration integration, quality control
Month 5–6: File petition + launch stay strategy
Month 7–11: Preliminary response period and institution watch
Month 11–12: Institution decision and forum recalibration
```

The practical rule is simple: **win the first 180 days of IPR planning, or you spend the next 18 months recovering from preventable defects.**

---

## 10. Multi-Petition Strategy and Claim Allocation

When asserted claims and patent count are large, one petition is often structurally insufficient.

### Allocation rules

- put highest-value asserted independent claims in Petition 1
- isolate alternative claim-construction theories in separate petitions
- avoid overloading any petition with low-probability backup grounds
- maintain a clear rationale for each petition boundary (helps with discretionary review optics)

### Internal trigger for second petition

Open a second-petition track when either condition is met:

1. challenged claim count exceeds practical word-limit coverage, or
2. best prior art theories are mutually inconsistent under one POSITA narrative

---

## 11. Expert Declaration Build System (Not a Last-Minute Attachment)

Strong declarations are built in layers, not drafted at the end.

### Four-layer workflow

1. **Technical layer:** element mapping and terminology harmonization
2. **Historical layer:** publication date proof and authenticity path
3. **POSITA layer:** skill-level assumptions validated across all grounds
4. **Argument layer:** motivation-to-combine logic with explicit evidentiary anchors

### Quality-control checks

- every limitation has a primary citation and at least one backup citation
- every obviousness rationale has a non-conclusory evidentiary basis
- no declaration paragraph conflicts with district court pleadings/contentions

---

## 12. Post-Institution Operating Plan

Institution is midpoint, not finish line.

### First 45 days after institution

- re-baseline district court and ITC messaging
- finalize deposition plan for patent owner expert(s)
- harden demonstratives for APJ comprehension, not jury persuasion
- run red-team review against your weakest ground

### Patent owner response counter-plan

Anticipate common patent owner moves:

- narrowing claim-construction proposals
- attacks on date/authentication and printed-publication status
- \"hindsight\" attacks on motivation to combine
- selective prosecution-history framing

Assign pre-drafted rebuttal owners before the patent owner response is filed.

---

## 13. Motion to Amend (MTA) Response Playbook

Even though amendment success rates remain limited, respondents should treat MTA as a serious merits track.

### MTA response checklist

- test substitute claims against written description support
- run rapid prior art search specific to substitute limitations
- identify new indefiniteness/enablement pressure points for parallel forum use
- update settlement valuation if substitute claims materially narrow exposure

If you ignore MTA until late briefing, you risk conceding commercially important claim scope by default.

---

## 14. Federal Circuit Appeal Readiness (From Day 1)

Build appeal readiness during PTAB proceedings.

### Preserve review-worthy issues

- claim-construction disputes with clean record support
- evidentiary rulings tied to institution or FWD outcomes
- legal interpretation issues that could affect estoppel scope or merits framework

### Record discipline rule

Maintain an indexed issue log mapping each major argument to:

- petition section
- declaration paragraph(s)
- reply/sur-reply treatment
- oral argument references

This lowers post-FWD scramble cost and improves appellate coherence.
