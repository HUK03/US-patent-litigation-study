# ITC Section 337 Defense Playbook (for Respondents)

> **Quick Reference:** ITC investigations move faster than district court patent cases and can lead to exclusion orders that block imports into the United States. For Korean companies shipping products into the US market, ITC risk requires a dedicated, parallel defense track from Day 1.

---

## 1. Why ITC Changes the Defense Posture

Unlike district court, the US International Trade Commission (ITC) cannot award monetary damages. But it can issue:

- **Limited Exclusion Orders (LEO):** blocks importation of infringing products by named respondents
- **General Exclusion Orders (GEO):** blocks importation by all entities for covered products (higher threshold)
- **Cease and Desist Orders (CDO):** restricts US inventory and downstream sales activities

For operating companies, especially export-driven Korean manufacturers, this can be more disruptive than damages exposure.

🌏 **Korean company lens:** ITC is often used strategically by US competitors against foreign supply chains. Assume business continuity risk immediately (customs stoppage, customer escalation, redesign pressure).

---

## 2. ITC Timeline and Critical Deadlines

A Section 337 case typically reaches evidentiary hearing in ~8–10 months and final ITC decision in ~15–18 months.

### Typical timeline snapshot

```
Complaint filed at ITC
   ↓
Institution of investigation (usually within ~30 days)
   ↓
Target date set (often 15–18 months from institution)
   ↓
Ground rules / procedural schedule
   ↓
Fact discovery + expert discovery (compressed)
   ↓
Evidentiary hearing before ALJ (bench trial style)
   ↓
Initial Determination (ID) by ALJ
   ↓
Commission review (optional, often partial)
   ↓
Final determination + remedy
   ↓
60-day Presidential review period
```

### Respondent Day-0 checklist

- [ ] Confirm **institution notice date** and identify all accused product models/SKUs
- [ ] Engage ITC-specific outside counsel (not just district-court team)
- [ ] Trigger ITC-focused litigation hold across US + Korea custodians
- [ ] Start import-flow mapping (HTS codes, importer of record, US warehouses)
- [ ] Initiate customs and supply-chain risk analysis
- [ ] Launch parallel non-infringement + invalidity workstream
- [ ] Decide whether to seek settlement window before procedural lock-in

## 3. Jurisdictional Gate: Domestic Industry (DI)

Complainant must prove a **domestic industry** related to the asserted patent:

- **Technical prong:** US domestic products practice at least one asserted claim
- **Economic prong:** significant US investment (plant/equipment, labor/capital, or licensing activity)

### Defensive use of DI

DI is frequently an underused defense lever. For NPE complainants, challenge:

- weak licensing-based DI evidence
- mismatch between licensed technology and asserted claims
- insufficient US nexus at the time required by the schedule

If DI fails, the complainant loses even if infringement is otherwise shown.

## 4. Core Defense Tracks in ITC

Build three tracks in parallel from the first month.

### Track A: Non-infringement and claim scope

- attack claim construction early and surgically
- prioritize dispositive elements for technical experts
- separate legacy vs. current product versions (version control matters)

### Track B: Invalidity and parallel PTAB strategy

- ITC cannot cancel patents; invalidity is a defense but patent survives unless separately invalidated
- run a coordinated PTAB strategy (IPR) when printed-publication prior art is strong
- maintain product/prior-use/on-sale invalidity theories in parallel forum planning

### Track C: Remedy defense (import-ban mitigation)

Even if violation is found, respondents must build a remedy-limitation record:

- **Public interest factors:** health/safety, competitive conditions, US consumers, US production
- **Narrow product scope arguments:** limit product coverage by feature/SKU/version
- **Certification feasibility:** show practical methods for Customs screening

---

## 5. Coordinating ITC and District Court Cases

Complainants often file ITC and district court actions together.

### Practical coordination rules

- treat ITC schedule as the pacing item (faster forum)
- align claim construction positions across forums unless strategic reason to diverge
- manage admissions carefully; deposition testimony in one forum will be used in the other
- use ITC technical record to pressure early global settlement if favorable

### Statutory stay note

For overlapping patent issues, district court claims against ITC respondents are often stayed while ITC proceeds. Plan for ITC as the main merits battlefield in year one.

---

## 6. Discovery and Evidence Control (Cross-Border)

ITC discovery is compressed and intense. Korean respondents should pre-build translation and document-control workflows.

### High-risk evidence buckets

- engineering specs and source code history
- BOM/supplier change records (version-by-version)
- import records (commercial invoices, entry summaries, entity roles)
- marketing statements that characterize product functionality

### Process controls for Korean companies

- appoint bilingual evidence leads (legal + engineering)
- standardize Korean-to-English technical term dictionary at project start
- lock a single product-version chronology used by all witnesses
- pre-brief witnesses on US deposition practice and record precision

---

## 7. Settlement, Consent Orders, and Business Continuity

### Settlement windows

- pre-institution/preliminary phase (highest confidentiality, most flexible)
- post-Markman-equivalent technical exchanges (better risk visibility)
- pre-hearing (high pressure, high cost already incurred)

### Consent order option

A consent order can resolve investigation exposure without full hearing but imposes compliance obligations. Use only when monitoring burden and downstream customer impact are acceptable.

### Business continuity planning

- prepare redesign roadmap with engineering deadlines tied to hearing schedule
- pre-negotiate contingency sourcing if import restrictions issue
- customer communication plan for potential customs hold scenarios

## 9. Executive Dashboard Metrics to Report Weekly

- % of accused SKUs with completed element-level mapping
- % of key custodians with completed collection + review
- DI attack readiness (technical/economic prong scores)
- import-ban exposure by revenue tier
- redesign feasibility and ETA by product family

A respondent that treats ITC as merely "fast district court" is usually behind by month two. ITC success depends on synchronized legal, technical, and supply-chain execution.

## 10. High-Risk Procedural Moments (Where Respondents Commonly Lose Ground)

### A. Early procedural submissions

Respondents often under-resource the first round of procedural submissions because merits work feels more urgent. This is a mistake. Early filings set:

- scope of accused products and technical issues
- discovery proportionality boundaries
- hearing calendar pressure points
- credibility with the ALJ on case management discipline

### B. Source code production protocol

Unclear version mapping and poor repository history extraction can damage both non-infringement and remedy defenses. Set a protocol that includes:

- immutable hash/version references
- release date table tied to import records
- role-based access logs for production integrity

### C. Witness preparation timing

Compressed ITC schedules mean late witness prep causes factual inconsistencies that are hard to repair. Complete first-round prep before expert reports lock.

---

## 11. Remedy and Customs Enforcement Readiness

ITC remedy analysis should begin before liability is decided.

### If an exclusion order risk becomes material

- map each accused SKU to import channels and customer contracts
- prepare a feature-level redesign declaration package
- define CBP-facing product identifiers (model/version/firmware signatures)
- draft customer communications for continuity scenarios

### Presidential review period (60 days)

During the Presidential review period, covered products may still enter under bond in many cases. Finance and logistics teams should pre-model:

- expected bond exposure
- shipment prioritization logic
- contractual pass-through risk

## 12. ITC–PTAB–District Court Decision Matrix

Use this matrix at executive review meetings.

| Scenario | ITC posture | PTAB posture | District court posture | Recommended defensive focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong non-infringement, weak invalidity | Favorable | Uncertain | Mixed | Push technical hearing narrative; narrow remedy scope; preserve settlement leverage |
| Mixed infringement risk, strong printed-publication art | Moderate risk | Favorable to IPR | Early-stage | File IPR early, seek stay where possible, maintain ITC remedy defenses |
| High infringement risk, weak PTAB art, strong product prior art | Unfavorable | Weak | Potentially favorable | Emphasize product/prior-use invalidity in court, redesign acceleration, remedy mitigation |
| Business-critical imports with customer concentration | High exposure | Any | Any | Prioritize continuity plan, customs strategy, and targeted settlement corridor |

## 13. Budget Framework and Staffing Model

### Respondent-side rough cost ranges (single investigation, moderate complexity)

| Workstream | Typical range |
|---|---|
| ITC core merits defense through hearing | $1.5M–$4.0M |
| Parallel technical expert work | $300K–$1.0M |
| Customs/remedy support and supply-chain modeling | $100K–$500K |
| Parallel IPR preparation (if filed) | $75K–$200K per petition |

### Staffing blueprint

- **Internal legal lead:** owns forum alignment and privilege architecture
- **Technical lead:** owns product-version truth set and expert interfaces
- **Supply-chain lead:** owns import data integrity and continuity execution
- **Executive sponsor:** enforces milestone decisions and budget gates

Treat ITC as a business-continuity case with legal consequences, not just a legal case with business side effects.
