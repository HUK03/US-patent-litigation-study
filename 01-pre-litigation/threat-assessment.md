# Threat Assessment: Demand Letters and Pre-Suit Contacts

> **Quick Reference:** When a patent demand letter arrives, **do not respond without counsel review**. Preserve all communications. Triage within 48 hours. This document tells you exactly what to do.

---

## 1. Types of Pre-Suit Contact

Before a patent lawsuit is filed, potential plaintiffs typically make one or more forms of pre-suit contact. Understanding the type of contact helps determine the appropriate response.

| Contact Type | Description | Key Risks | Typical Sender |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cease and Desist (C&D) Letter** | Demands you stop making/selling/using a product | Creates knowledge of patent (willfulness risk); may trigger DJ jurisdiction | NPE, competitor |
| **Licensing Demand Letter** | Offers license; demands royalties without demanding you stop | Establishes "notice" for pre-suit damages; willfulness risk | NPE/PAE, large portfolio holders |
| **Notice Letter** | Provides notice of patent, intentionally ambiguous | Sets date for pre-suit damages calculation; creates willfulness knowledge | SEP holders, NPEs |
| **Industry-Wide Campaign Letter** | Mass mailing to entire industry | Often low-quality patents; high chance of invalidity; indicates NPE playbook | PAE, NPE |
| **Portfolio License Demand** | Demands license to entire patent portfolio | Complex — requires portfolio analysis; settlement pressure is real | Large tech companies, universities |

### Warning Signs of Aggressive NPE Tactics
- Multiple defendants named in separate but identical suits (serial litigation campaign)
- Filed in Eastern District of Texas or Western District of Texas
- Broad, vague claim of infringement with no specific claim chart
- Tight settlement demand deadline ("respond within 30 days or we file")
- Reference to recent verdicts or settlements to anchor damages expectations

## 2. Immediate Response Protocol — First 48 Hours Checklist

Upon receipt of any patent demand letter or notice of potential infringement:

**Legal/IP Team Actions:**
- [ ] **Do NOT respond** to the demand letter without review by patent litigation counsel
- [ ] **Do NOT destroy** any communications related to the letter or accused technology
- [ ] **Forward all materials** to in-house IP counsel and litigation counsel immediately
- [ ] **Calendar the response deadline** stated in the letter (even if it is not a legal deadline)
- [ ] **Preserve the original letter** (physical + electronic), including envelope with postmark
- [ ] Determine whether a complaint has already been filed (check PACER: pacer.gov)
- [ ] If a complaint has been filed: **calendar 21-day answer deadline** (note: extensions routinely granted by stipulation, but must be requested promptly)
- [ ] **Assess IPR deadline:** Begin counting the 365-day clock from date of service of any complaint

**Business Unit Actions:**
- [ ] Identify the accused product(s) and the business unit responsible
- [ ] Brief the relevant product/engineering team through counsel (to maintain privilege)
- [ ] **Do not** have engineers or product teams contact the patent holder directly
- [ ] **Do not** create new written analyses of the patent without direction from counsel
- [ ] Do not alter or delete existing product documentation

**Communications Protocol:**
- [ ] Route all external communications through counsel
- [ ] Mark all internal communications with "Attorney-Client Privileged — Prepared at Request of Counsel"
- [ ] Do not discuss the matter via messaging apps (KakaoTalk, Slack, Teams) without privilege guidance

🌏 **Korean Company Note:** Korean engineers and managers may not be familiar with US litigation hold obligations. Urgently brief all relevant personnel (including Korea-based staff working on the accused product) that US litigation discovery can require production of **all** communications, including Korean-language messages and emails. See [Litigation Hold](litigation-hold-evidence-preservation.md).

## 3. Threat Triage Decision Framework

Use this decision tree to assess the severity and appropriate response to a patent threat. Complete within 5–7 business days.

```
Step 1: Is the patent real and in force?
  ├── Verify patent number on USPTO.gov (Google Patents)
  ├── Check status: Is it expired? Abandoned? Subject to a terminal disclaimer?
  └── Has it previously been challenged at PTAB (IPR/PGR history)?

Step 2: Who is the claimant?
  ├── NPE/PAE?
  │   ├── Research litigation history (Lex Machina, RPX, Docket Navigator)
  │   ├── What is their file-vs-settle rate? Average settlement amount?
  │   └── Do they have a history of taking cases to trial? (Most NPEs do NOT)
  └── Operating company (competitor)?
      ├── Do they have relevant patent portfolio we could cross-license?
      └── Is this part of a broader competitive dispute?

Step 3: Is the accused product/feature genuinely at risk?
  ├── Map the patent claims against your product/process
  ├── Is there a colorable claim of infringement? (Honest assessment)
  ├── Are alternative non-infringing designs readily available?
  └── Is the accused feature core (high value) or peripheral (low value)?

Step 4: What is the potential damages exposure?
  ├── Identify the accused product's US revenue (past 6 years)
  ├── Estimate a reasonable royalty rate range (Georgia-Pacific framework)
  ├── Consider enhanced damages risk (willfulness — was there prior notice?)
  └── Is injunctive relief a realistic risk? (NPEs rarely obtain post-eBay)

Step 5: Prior art assessment
  ├── Are there published prior art references predating the patent's priority date?
  ├── Check company's own prior publications, products, and presentations
  ├── Consider § 101 Alice/Mayo challenge (software/AI/business method patents)
  └── Review prosecution history for claim narrowing (estoppel issues)
```

## 5. Researching the Claimant and Patent

### Claimant Background Research

**Litigation history databases** (require subscription or outside counsel access):
- **Lex Machina** (legal analytics): Case outcomes, settlement rates, judge assignment history
- **Docket Navigator**: Docket search, claim construction history, damage awards
- **RPX Insight**: NPE-specific intelligence, portfolio relationships, litigation patterns
- **PACER** (pacer.gov): Free docket search for actual filings

Key metrics to gather:
- How many cases has this claimant filed in the past 5 years?
- What percentage settled? At what typical stage?
- Have they ever taken a case to trial? What was the outcome?
- Are other large companies also receiving identical demands? (Industry campaign?)
- Who are their litigation funders (if determinable)?

### Patent Background Research

**Free resources:**
- **Google Patents** (patents.google.com): Full patent text, prosecution history, citation network
- **USPTO Patent Center** (patentcenter.uspto.gov): Prosecution history (file wrapper) download
- **Espacenet** (worldwide patent families): Related foreign patents

**Key research points:**
- **Prosecution history:** Were claims narrowed during prosecution? What arguments did the applicant make? (Prosecution history estoppel limits doctrine of equivalents)
- **Patent family:** Are there continuation applications still pending? Future scope risk?
- **Prior art citations in prosecution:** What art did the examiner consider? What was NOT considered?
- **Assignment history:** Is the claimant actually the owner? (Check USPTO assignment database)
- **Standard Essential Patent (SEP) status:** Is the patent declared essential to a standard (Wi-Fi, LTE, 5G, USB, HEVC)? If so, FRAND licensing obligations may apply

## 6. Declaratory Judgment Strategy

In some situations, a defendant may benefit from **proactively filing a declaratory judgment (DJ) action** seeking a ruling of non-infringement and/or invalidity before the patent holder files its own suit.

### When to Consider a DJ Action

- You have received a demand letter and believe suit is inevitable
- You have a preferred venue (e.g., N.D. Cal. or D. Del.) that would be more defendant-friendly than where the plaintiff is likely to file
- The patentee is running an industry-wide campaign and you want to be first in your preferred jurisdiction
- You need to "race to the courthouse" to establish jurisdiction before the plaintiff's preferred filing date

### Legal Requirements (*MedImmune v. Genentech*, Supreme Court 2007)
A DJ plaintiff must demonstrate a **"case or controversy"** — meaning there must be a genuine, immediate dispute about the patent's validity or applicability. Receipt of a detailed demand letter asserting infringement of specific claims is generally sufficient. Exploratory licensing discussions alone may not be sufficient.

### Key Risks of Filing a DJ Action
- **Accelerates the dispute** — may prompt the patent holder to immediately file their own suit in their preferred venue (and if they file first, your DJ action may be dismissed)
- **Eliminates settlement flexibility** — filing a lawsuit signals aggressive posture; may poison licensing negotiations
- **Creates discovery obligations** — once in litigation, document preservation and production obligations are immediate

### 🌏 Foreign Company DJ Considerations
For Korean or other foreign companies, a DJ action filed in a favorable US district (e.g., N.D. Cal. or D. Del.) **before the patent holder files in E.D. Texas** can be particularly valuable, since foreign companies cannot rely on TC Heartland to defeat EDTX venue. Timing is critical — once the patent holder files in EDTX, it is difficult (though not impossible via § 1404(a) transfer) to move the case to a more favorable venue.

## 7. Business Unit Engagement Without Waiving Privilege

A common mistake is having engineers and product teams conduct their own patent analysis and document it in emails or slide decks — without going through legal counsel. This creates:

1. **Non-privileged documents** subject to production in discovery (the engineer's email saying "we might infringe claim 5" is discoverable)
2. **Willfulness risk** — documented awareness of infringement risk without mitigation strengthens a willfulness claim

**Best practices for engaging engineers:**
- All communications about the patent should be **addressed to and from IP/legal counsel**, not engineer-to-engineer
- Technical questions should be framed as: "Please provide the following information for my legal analysis" (from counsel to engineer)
- Engineers should **not** conduct their own independent patent claim charts or write opinions about infringement
- Face-to-face or verbal briefings (documented only by counsel's notes) are preferable to written communications
- Mark all materials: "Prepared at the Request of Counsel — Attorney-Client Privileged"
